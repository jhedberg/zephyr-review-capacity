What the terms, choices and colours mean

Terms

Listed
The Maintainers and Collaborators of the area in MAINTAINERS.yml. "The list" always means these people.
Regular reviewer
A listed person who answers at least half of five or more review requests in the period.
Occasional and silent
A listed person who took part but less than that, or not at all.
Assignee
The person whose approval a merge needs, usually a Maintainer. Assignees are added and changed by hand, so the page takes whoever is assigned now, and counts an area's wait only where one of its own Maintainers is assigned.
Candidate
Someone who is not listed for the area but reviewed more of its pull requests than at least half of the listed people did. Fewer than five reviews never count.

Review cover: the state of an area

No regular
Nobody on the list answers at least half of the review requests they get.
One regular
One listed person does. No pull request can have two regular reviewers.
Thin
Two or more do, but they also write much of the code: a fifth or more of the pull requests have fewer than two of them besides the author.
Covered
Nearly every pull request has two regular reviewers besides its author.

Maintainer: reviews, compared with others

Reviews most
A Maintainer reviewed at least as many of the area's pull requests as anyone else.
Second or Writes most
Someone else reviewed more, and the Maintainer is a regular reviewer or writes half or more of the area's pull requests, which nobody can review for themselves.
Behind
Someone else reviewed more and the Maintainer is not a regular reviewer. Only reviews are counted: triage, design and release work do not show here.
No Maintainer
The area lists none.

Colours

On the two states
Review cover: red means no or one regular reviewer, green covered, grey thin. Maintainer: red means none or behind, green reviews the most, grey in between. These are fixed meanings.
Worst tenth and best tenth
In "PRs/week per regular", "Open now (change)", "2nd approval" and the two "Waiting" columns, the marks compare an area with the other areas in scope. A mark is left out when too many areas share the value for a tenth to mean anything.
No colour elsewhere
In the percentage columns most areas sit at 0% or 100%, so a best or worst tenth would be arbitrary there.

The Maintainer

Only reviews are counted here; triage, design and release work do not show. A merge needs an approval from an assignee, who is usually a Maintainer. Assignees are added and changed by hand, so this takes whoever is assigned now, and counts only pull requests on which one of this area's Maintainers is assigned.

Review cover

A pull request needs two approvals from people other than its author. A regular reviewer is a listed person who answers at least half of five or more requests in the period. The bar counts, for each pull request that became ready, the regular reviewers besides its author; with fewer than two, the pull request relies on occasional or unlisted reviewers for an approval. It follows the period and the counting options above.

How this is counted

  • An area is a section of MAINTAINERS.yml; its pull requests are those carrying the area's own label. Areas that share a label share their pull requests.
  • Review requests come from the project's assigner (scripts/ci/set_assignees.py): when a pull request is out of draft it asks every Maintainer and Collaborator of every area the changed files belong to, without a cap. Someone it cannot formally request, because they are not a repository collaborator, is asked with an @-mention in a comment; that counts as a request here. It does not ask someone who has reviewed already, so a listed person's review without a request counts as asked and answered.
  • A request counts once per person and pull request, on the day of the first request. It counts as answered when the person reviews at or after the request and before the end of the period. A comment counts as an answer too, unless that is switched off.
  • Not counted for or against anyone: the person's own pull requests, requests withdrawn by someone else without an answer, pull requests closed with less than three ready days after the request, and open ones with less than seven ready days since the request. Draft time is not ready time.
  • A miss is excused, and so not counted, when the pull request closed within seven days of the request or two other people had approved it within seven days, unless that is switched off.
  • A request the person withdrew themselves is called declined; the assigner does not ask them again. It counts like a request left unanswered when the pull request is this area's, which the page takes to be the case when one of the area's Maintainers is the assignee: a listed person is expected to review the area's own pull requests. On other pull requests, where the area is only touched in passing, declining is not counted for or against anyone. This can be switched off. Read such a figure with care: someone who declines many of an area's own pull requests may show that the area is drawn too wide for them, for example when part of it is a technology their work does not touch. The remedy then is a finer split of the area in MAINTAINERS.yml, not a verdict on the person.
  • These three choices, and one to leave out pull requests that also carry an area the person maintains, are check boxes under "One area", next to the period. "All areas" always counts with comments as answers, with the excuse rule and with declined requests on the area's own pull requests as unanswered, because its figures are worked out once for every area when the data is refreshed.
  • People with fewer than five countable requests are not judged. The line never takes a Maintainer off.
  • Approved counts the pull requests the person first approved in the period, asked or not. Bot accounts are left out everywhere.
  • A regular reviewer is a listed person who answers at least half of five or more requests in the period. An occasional reviewer took part but is not regular; a silent one did not take part; with fewer than five requests there is too little to tell. A pull request is not covered when fewer than two regular reviewers are left besides its author. An area whose label is named after another area (a West project using "area: Tests", say) sees that area's pull requests, so its figures are not its own. The same holds for the areas MAINTAINERS.yml marks as meta (Documentation, Tests, Samples, Release, Benchmarks): the assigner applies their label and asks their people on any pull request that touches their files, and itself never counts them as short of reviewers. Both kinds are hidden under "All areas" unless asked for.
  • The assignee is picked by the assigner as well: a Maintainer, other than the author, of the non-platform area with the most changed files, with platform areas as a fallback and meta areas only when nothing else is touched. People change or add assignees by hand afterwards; the page uses the assignees a pull request has now.
  • A period that includes a holiday season lowers everyone's rate. Compare like with like before drawing a conclusion about a person.
  • The numbers come from public GitHub data and are refreshed by a script; see the source for the exact rules.
